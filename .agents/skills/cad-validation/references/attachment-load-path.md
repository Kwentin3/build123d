# Attachment and load path

Use when an identified feature retains/locates a part or carries a specified
load, or a change affects that joint's supporting material. A drainage opening
does not activate this pattern merely because it is circular.

Establish the joint before sizing holes: connected parts and operating state;
fastener/stud/insert/hook or other retention mechanism; relevant head, nut,
washer, thread/grip dimensions; accessible sides and tools; bearing/clamping
surfaces; load directions and material/process where capacity is in scope.
Separate supplied requirements from design choices. Ask for decisive missing
inputs rather than inventing hardware from the hole diameter.

Consider how the function could be lost: head/nut cannot seat, tool cannot
reach, insufficient engagement, a supporting region is removed, the joint
cannot be assembled or serviced, or the load has no intended path to its
support. Evaluate only relevant mechanisms.

Use the model's native geometry for alignment, through paths, hardware/tool
envelopes, bearing patches, local surrounding material and connection to the
support. Inspect adjacent cavities and the complete relevant depth, including
the actual bearing location. State the measured region and geometric criterion.
A local annular probe is evidence about that region, not proof of joint strength.

An intersecting cavity may be intentional. A through stud supported at another
surface and a screw clamping directly onto this region need different checks.
Do not require a closed cylindrical wall, a full annulus or any universal
ligament/edge-distance ratio without a functional and source-backed criterion.

Examples of conclusions: misalignment preventing specified assembly is FAIL;
clear geometry for the selected hardware is PASS for that geometry; unknown
hardware/load/material means dependent retention/capacity is NOT_CHECKED.
An unexplained breakout is an engineering observation until its function and
acceptance criterion are established. Strength, pullout, preload, friction,
fatigue and wall-anchor capacity require applicable calculation or physical
evidence; geometry alone does not supply them.

Basis: [NASA RP-1228](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19900009424), especially
printed pp.21–22; function/failure adaptation and source limits are documented
in [research](../../../../docs/engineering-review/sources.md).
